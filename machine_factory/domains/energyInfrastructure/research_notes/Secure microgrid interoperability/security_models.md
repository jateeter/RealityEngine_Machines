# Secure microgrid operations and model integrations

Research date: 2026-11-24. Research notes for the factory package, not an implemented security system or a conformance assessment. All architecture and acceptance prescriptions below are proposed domain requirements. No field devices, live models, gateways or transport links were probed; no software was installed, and no actuation was attempted. Public project existence and selected license declarations were checked, not particular builds' fitness or maintenance SLAs. NIST SP 1108 PDF interpretation is covered by the grid-standards researcher.

## Which security standards and public implementations apply?

### Takeaway

Use a layered baseline: smart-grid relationship risk (NISTIR 7628), operational technology engineering (SP 800-82), risk governance and continuous monitoring (CSF/RMF), IACS system/component assurance (IEC 62443), and protocol-specific power-system security (IEC 62351). Secure communications are an entry precondition, while endpoint compromise, unsafe authorized commands and loss of operational observability remain separate risks.

### Inferences

- Proposed security zones: protection/IED and local primary controls; DER/BMS/inverter controllers; supervisory microgrid EMS/SCADA; OT DMZ/protocol gateways; enterprise/utility/aggregator interfaces; model/RAG/agent services; maintenance and security management. Keep command paths distinct from observability paths. Assign actual zone names and SL-T after site risk analysis.
- Every conduit needs owner, source/destination identities, protocol/profile/edition, function/role, direction, latency budget, freshness rules, fallback, authorization and proof of secure-channel establishment. “All channels secure” is a commissioning condition, not a test result.
- Fast protection, synch-check and primary voltage/frequency control remain deterministic local functions. Supervisory orchestration can request bounded actions; LLM latency or availability must never be a dependency of safety action.

### Gaps

Licensed IEC/IEEE texts, site topology, authority/jurisdiction, utility interconnection agreement, selected equipment firmware/protocol capabilities, risk appetite and plant timing budgets remain uncollected. A global domain should support profiles; it cannot claim every national regulatory regime at once. Independent standards conformity or safety certification requires appropriate engineering/test bodies and physical evidence.

## How should dynamic threat assessment and deterministic safety enforcement work?

### Takeaway

Maintain separate cyber-risk, evidence-quality and electrical-operating states. A compromised or unknown observation must not become a healthy zero-bit. Recommendations are advisory; a local deterministic command gate controls actuation and independently verifies the result.

Proposed deterministic evidence loop (not prescribed formula from NIST):

1. Ingest allowlisted signed/authenticated telemetry, device inventory/configuration, certificate health, endpoint audit events, protocol-gateway decisions, passive-network alerts, approved topology revisions, approved maintenance, weather/hazard and threat-intelligence feed versions. Record producer identity, event/receive time, sequence, asset, quality and provenance hash.
2. Validate schema, canonical asset mapping, units/sign, range, freshness, ordering, calibration, clock quality and anti-replay; mark stale, conflicting, synthetic and missing data explicitly. Preserve last valid value with age; never represent unknown as normal.
3. Correlate deterministic detections with equipment reachability, firmware/SBOM/VEX, exploitation evidence, operating mode, active constraints, community-critical loads and control health. Threat intelligence expiration or missing feeds reduces assessment confidence; it does not prove low risk.
4. Record scenario-specific likelihood and consequence with rationale, evidence references, confidence and expiry. Consider human safety, protection integrity, loss of service, restoration time, privacy and environmental impact separately. Initial qualitative scales and thresholds require engineering-owner approval; do not multiply invented scores into claimed compliance.
5. Evaluate independent dimensions: `evidenceState={valid,degraded,unknown,conflicting}`, `cyberPosture={normal,suspect,restricted,incident}`, `operatingMode={gridConnected,transitionToIsland,islanded,blackStart,resynchronizing,recovery}`. A configured policy maps these plus action class into allow, bounded allow, hold-for-review or block. Hard safety inhibits override economic/carbon optimization.
6. Restrict/revoke compromised capabilities, quarantine affected observation channels and require independent readback; retain local protection. Isolation, load shedding and failover follow a pre-engineered mode-specific plan, not a generic “turn everything off.” An LLM may draft an incident explanation but cannot directly quarantine a feeder.
7. Escalate with operator, OT security and utility roles; log who accepted residual risk and for how long. Restore from verified configuration, validate physical topology/sensors and credentials, rehearse rollback, and reopen automation only after signed closeout and recovery evidence.
8. Reassess on topology/mode changes, new threat evidence, changed firmware/model/policy/RAG revisions, certificate events and configured timers. Use rate limiting and hysteresis to avoid alarm-driven control oscillation. Store prior and new decisions and policy version for replay.

Proposed threat-to-test inventory:

| Scenario | Deterministic evidence / gate | Expected safe behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Compromised but authenticated DER reports false SOC | BMS/independent meter consistency, provenance and plausibility checks | Mark conflicting; stop discretionary dispatch from that estimate; preserve reserve/local BMS protection. |
| Stale/out-of-order/replayed telemetry | Source sequence, event age, receipt age, time-quality checks | Reject replay; degrade evidence; block actions requiring fresh values. |
| GNSS/PTP/NTP manipulation | Clock-quality alarms, holdover, cross-clock comparison | Disable time-sensitive synchronization decisions when invalid; local protection persists. |
| Rogue master/privilege abuse | Asset identity, conduit allowlist, subject-action-resource policy | Deny unauthorized writes even over an encrypted authenticated connection. |
| Authorized command with wrong units/sign | Typed schema, UCUM-unit map, bounds and per-asset capability | Reject before adapter; record exact reason. |
| TOCTOU topology/reserve change | Topology/config/state revision precondition checked immediately before execution | Reject expired precondition and replan; prior approval cannot authorize a changed operation. |
| Coordinated malicious flexible-load dispatch | Aggregate ramp/feeder/critical-load envelope plus rate constraints | Deny unsafe aggregate even when each request individually fits a device bound. |
| Certificate revocation/key compromise | Revocation/rotation and identity policy health | Revoke relevant command capability; safe local operation and approved recovery route. |
| Firmware or relay-setting drift | Signed inventory, approved baseline and maintenance window | Hold remote changes; require engineering review and certified procedure. |
| Cyber alarm and model outage during islanding | Local mode state, supervisory timeout and incident policy | Continue pre-engineered local island controls; no LLM-generated switching sequence. |
| Incomplete actuation after network loss | Dispatch ID, adapter ACK, timeout, independent state readback | State `completionUnknown`; no blind retry/non-idempotent repeat; reconcile before compensation. |
| Forged model completion/synthetic success | Completion source identity, dispatch binding, synthetic marker, readback evidence | Model message never establishes field completion; synthetic record cannot clear real action or acceptance. |
| Poisoned RAG asks for feeder opening/exfiltration | Quarantine/review, retrieval scope, tool allowlist, egress deny | Retrieve as untrusted data; no new permission or command; alert and retain audit. |
| Compromised update/container/model | Signature identity/digest, SBOM and offline scan/test evidence | Refuse unapproved artifact; preserve known-good operational configuration. |
| Alert/log flood | Per-source budgets, bounded queues, disk health, security-watchdog evidence | Preserve safety and essential control capacity; backpressure lower-priority analytics. |

Command gate contract proposal: authenticate principal and delegation chain; authorize exact asset/action; verify command schema, units, operating mode, topology/configuration version, freshness of required sensors, deterministic feasibility and electrical limits, active reservations/locks, approval-bound action hash, target capability and risk policy; enforce issue/expiry/not-before times, unique command/dispatch IDs, anti-replay and idempotency; append acceptance receipt; execute through a bounded adapter; verify independent readback and completion evidence. Sign/attest receipts and bind them to policy, model, evidence and command hashes. Use compare-and-swap/transactional fencing against concurrent agents. Authorization and physical feasibility are independent checks.

Cancellation must identify the last safe interrupt point; do not promise rollback of an irreversible breaker operation, energy discharge or physical fault. Compensation is a new assessed action under the current plant state. Emergency behavior is a pre-engineered local safety procedure with audit and recovery, never a model-granted bypass. Planned remote switching, resynchronization, relay setting changes, firmware updates and protection changes require the applicable approved operating/utility procedure and authorized humans.

### Inferences

Suggested security acceptance cases should inject the above threats into isolated deterministic fixtures and hardware/simulator scenarios. Every command-rejection case must prove zero adapter invocations, persisted deny reason and preserved local protection. Every approved bounded action must show correct asset, units, limits, exactly-once logical dispatch semantics, readback or explicit unknown completion. Risk reassessment must reproduce the same decision from pinned evidence and policy; a stale or unsigned input must not improve posture. Approval withdrawal/expiry and privilege revocation must take effect at execution time.

### Gaps

The clone has no evidenced dynamic risk service, signed threat feeds, command authorization service, calibrated sensor-quality thresholds, risk-to-state mapping, certified safety envelope or microgrid-specific cyber scenario execution receipts. ENX209's binary input contract does not by itself encode unknown/freshness/expiry; the factory must design explicit quality gates and sources before treating it as a complete security assessment. Security offline fixtures and three-engine parity are separate from protocol conformance, HIL protection tests and actual plant safety.

## How should agents, large models and RAG integrate securely?

### Takeaway

Reuse the established PE dispatch, OpenClaw/localAI inference and asset lifecycle boundaries, but introduce a microgrid-specific deterministic authorization/feasibility gateway. Models explain, retrieve and propose; only approved bounded workflows can reach command adapters. Fast controls do not depend on models.

Proposed roles and capabilities:

| Role | Allowed outputs/tools | Explicitly excluded |
|---|---|---|
| Standards/RAG steward | Submit allowlisted source candidates, extraction and citation-quality findings | Self-approve authoritative sources or grant retrieval access |
| Operations analyst | Read scoped recent observations, retrieve approved procedures, explain alarms | Raw vector writes, switches, device shells or direct field credentials |
| Forecast/optimization analyst | Propose typed load/DER/storage schedules; invoke bounded deterministic solver in sandbox | Invent sensor values, constraints or accepted grid setpoints |
| Cyber-risk analyst | Correlate supplied threat evidence, draft scenario/risk rationale and incident case | Assign final risk acceptance, silently quarantine or reconfigure protection |
| Dispatch planner | Assemble command proposal from validated schedule and current evidence | Execute its own unreviewed physical action |
| Deterministic execution service | Authorize and execute only approved bounded command classes; return receipts/readback | Open-ended model-generated shell/API URLs, policy mutation |
| Independent reviewer/operator | Approve signed command hash/revision under assigned role and separation of duties | Reuse stale approval after topology/policy/action change |
| Assurance/recovery analyst | Collect receipts, compare expected/observed result, draft closeout | Assert acceptance from model confidence or synthetic completion |

Workflow: authenticated field adapter → PE normalization and evidence validation → RE deterministic CES/arbitration → dispatch envelope and case → scoped retrieval/inference → typed proposal and deterministic simulation/constraint checks → authorized review where needed → just-in-time local policy/feasibility gateway → bounded field adapter → device ACK plus independent observed readback → PE completion/RE recovery → immutable evidence asset. Event/duty lanes, document handoffs and agent completion are distinct channels; free-text must not become native bus authority.

Integration requirements:

- Scope every session, tool token and retrieval query to site, equipment set, case/frame, continuation, role, allowed operation and expiration. Use separate identities for planning, execution, evidence writing and review. Tools enforce authorization server-side; model prompt text is not a security boundary.
- Keep grid/relay/device credentials out of model context and out of repositories. Tool manifests are allowlisted, reviewed and hash-pinned; default deny arbitrary URLs, filesystem access, shell, package installation and outbound messaging. No general browser or MCP tool receives actuator authority.
- Treat model/tool responses as hostile serialized data: size/depth/range limits, required types, units and enumerated actions; reject extra fields and executable strings. No `eval`, shell interpolation or ad hoc function discovery. Validate model output even if constrained generation is available.
- RAG corpus admission is separate from retrieval. Require licensed use, publisher/source identity, immutable bytes/hash, edition/effective date, jurisdiction, site/equipment applicability, confidentiality, approval role/time, supersession/staleness, extraction quality and section/page chunk lineage. Isolate normative public sources, licensed texts, site-private operational records, threat intelligence, and synthetic/evaluation content.
- Enforce site/role/license/source-status metadata filters before retrieval and at document access. Embeddings inherit source confidentiality; do not mix tenants in an unfiltered collection. The retriever returns revision/page citations and evidence age; the response must abstain on missing/conflicting authority and distinguish mandatory local procedure from public guidance.
- A retrieved PDF or runbook never creates instructions or tool privileges. Scan uploads, disable embedded scripts/macros/remote fetches, quarantine suspicious directives, human-review critical changes and preserve original file separately from extraction. Injection classifiers can help triage but are not sufficient authorization controls.
- Approved RAG changes create immutable revisions; dependencies, prompts, evaluations and conclusions become stale when relevant sources change. Keep retrieval/model/evidence digests per answer. Do not let agents write their own evidence back into the trusted operational collection without independent review.
- Run model service in a restricted zone with egress controls, resource budgets, retention/redaction and no raw tenant secrets in traces. Pin model/embedding/tokenizer/runner artifacts and prompt/tool/policy versions; record model-weight license separately. Do not enable remote code from model repositories by default.
- Evaluate wrong-unit proposals, unsafe optimizations, false citations, cross-tenant retrieval, stale procedures, injection through documents/tool results/memory, tool escalation, resource exhaustion and model outage. Report task validity and groundedness, not just fluent answers. Keep synthetic completions clearly labeled and excluded from real dispatch success/acceptance evidence.
- On inference/retrieval failure, continue deterministic local operation and current approved schedule within its validity; hold new discretionary proposals and escalate. Cloud fallback requires an explicit deployment/data-processing choice; never silently send private grid data to another model provider.

### Inferences

Acceptance should progress from offline source/policy fixtures through isolated model/tool tests to simulator/HIL and staged supervised operation. Require 100% deny on the enumerated critical unauthorized-actuation tests, zero cross-scope retrieval leakage on the fixed test set, traceable citations for every normative claim in the curated evaluation set, observed physical constraints for every simulated schedule, bounded service timeouts and proof local safety remains functional during total AI loss. Statistical quality targets for noncritical explanation/forecasting must be selected against representative cases and documented uncertainty; they do not replace the hard command gate.

### Gaps

No microgrid-specific model benchmark, authorized energy collection, per-role retrieval/tool policy, artifact digest inventory or field-command gateway was implemented or tested during this research. Existing source boundaries are reusable design inputs only. Live model/tool behavior, endpoint identity, secure-channel probes and owner-accepted residual transport gaps require separate admission receipts before any integration is described as operationally secure.
